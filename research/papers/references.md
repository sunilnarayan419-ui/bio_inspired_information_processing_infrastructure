# Research References & Literature Roadmap

## Bio-Inspired Information Processing Infrastructure

> **Purpose:** Build a scientifically defensible computational abstraction of alternative pre-mRNA splicing and investigate whether selected biological principles can inspire fault-tolerant, context-dependent information-processing infrastructure.

---

# 1. How to Use This Literature Repository

This project is not based on reading papers merely to collect citations.

Each paper should answer a specific research question.

The reading workflow is:

```text
Read
  ↓
Understand
  ↓
Extract mechanism
  ↓
Identify constraints
  ↓
Compare with other evidence
  ↓
Build biological model
  ↓
Abstract computational principle
  ↓
Design software mechanism
```

Do **not** reverse the process:

```text
Build software first
      ↓
Find biology that sounds similar
```

That would create post-hoc biological justification.

---

# 3. Layer 1 — Alternative Splicing Fundamentals

## 3.1 Nilsen & Graveley (2010)

### Paper

**Nilsen, T. W., & Graveley, B. R. (2010).  
Expansion of the eukaryotic proteome by alternative splicing.  
Nature, 463, 457–463.**

### DOI

https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08909

### Why read it?

This is a foundational paper for understanding why alternative splicing is biologically important.

It introduces the relationship between:

```text
Pre-mRNA
   ↓
Alternative processing
   ↓
Different mature RNA isoforms
   ↓
Potentially different functional products
```

### Read for

- definition of alternative splicing
- types of alternative splicing
- relationship between RNA isoforms and protein products
- biological significance
- diversity generated by alternative processing

### Extract

```text
What is an isoform?
What is an alternative splicing event?
What types of alternative processing occur?
What does alternative splicing actually change?
```

### Do NOT conclude

> "Alternative splicing means redundancy."

That is an engineering interpretation, not the biological definition.

# 28. How to Read a Paper Efficiently

Do not read every paper linearly from page 1 to the end.

Use a five-pass strategy.

## Pass 1 — Abstract

Answer:

```text
What problem?
What mechanism?
What conclusion?
```

Time:

**5–10 minutes**

---

## Pass 2 — Figures

Look at:

- diagrams
- pathway models
- experimental designs
- graphs
- tables

Ask:

> What is the mechanism the authors are actually demonstrating?

Time:

**10–20 minutes**

---

## Pass 3 — Introduction

Read enough to understand:

```text
Known
 ↓
Unknown
 ↓
Research question
```

Do not memorize every background statement.

---

## Pass 4 — Results

This is where you spend most of your time.

For each experiment:

```text
Hypothesis
 ↓
Method
 ↓
Observation
 ↓
Interpretation
```

Write these four lines in your notes.

---

## Pass 5 — Discussion

Ask:

```text
What did they actually prove?

What did they infer?

What remains uncertain?

What limitations did they acknowledge?
```

This distinction is extremely important for your project.

# 30. Biological-to-Computational Translation Protocol

For every proposed mapping:

```text
BIOLOGICAL OBSERVATION
        ↓
MECHANISM
        ↓
FUNCTION
        ↓
CONSTRAINT
        ↓
COMPUTATIONAL ABSTRACTION
        ↓
ENGINEERING IMPLEMENTATION
        ↓
EXPERIMENT
```

Example:

```text
Alternative processing
        ↓
Regulated splice-site selection
        ↓
Different mature RNA isoforms
        ↓
Selection is constrained by sequence/context/regulators
        ↓
Controlled selection among valid processing pathways
        ↓
Context-aware routing engine
        ↓
Compare routing performance under controlled failures
```

This is a **hypothesis**, not a biological equivalence.

# 31. Evidence Classification

Every statement in the project should be classified as one of:

### BIOLOGICAL FACT

Directly supported by experimental evidence.

### SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION

Supported by multiple studies but involves interpretation.

### COMPUTATIONAL ABSTRACTION

An engineering representation of a biological principle.

### ENGINEERING DECISION

A design choice made for software reasons.

### HYPOTHESIS

Something that must be experimentally tested.

### SPECULATION

Potential future direction without sufficient evidence.

Use these labels in research notes whenever ambiguity exists.

---


# 38. Central Principle

The goal is **not**:

> "Build software that looks like biology."

The goal is:

> **"Investigate whether a rigorously extracted principle from biological information processing can provide a useful and measurable design principle for backend infrastructure."**

That distinction defines the research quality of this project.
